The Complete Guide

ATS Resume Writing Guide

Everything you need to know about ATS systems, scoring, and how to optimize your resume for maximum visibility.

What Is an ATS System?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software used by employers and recruiters to manage, filter, and rank job applications. Over 98% of Fortune 500 companies and 75% of all mid-to-large companies use ATS software to screen candidates before a human recruiter ever sees the resume.

ATS platforms like Workday, Greenhouse, iCIMS, Taleo, and Lever automatically scan resumes for keywords, formatting signals, and section completeness. They assign scores to each application, and only the highest-scoring resumes reach a recruiter's desk.

If your resume isn't optimized for ATS, it may never be seen by a human — regardless of how qualified you are. This is why using a free ATS resume checker like this one is essential before submitting any application.

How ATS Software Works

When you submit a resume, the ATS software immediately parses the file — extracting text, identifying sections, and matching your content against the job description. Here's the typical process:

  • Parsing: The ATS reads your resume and extracts structured data — name, email, work history, skills, education.
  • Keyword Matching: It compares your content against required and preferred skills from the job description.
  • Scoring: Based on keyword density, section completeness, and formatting compliance, it assigns a rank score.
  • Filtering: Resumes below a threshold score are automatically rejected or buried.
  • Ranking: Remaining resumes are ranked for recruiter review.

ATS Resume Formatting Guide

Formatting is critical for ATS success. Many beautifully designed resumes fail ATS screening because they use elements the parser cannot read.

  • Use standard fonts like Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, or Garamond.
  • Avoid tables and columns — they confuse most ATS parsers.
  • No text boxes or headers/footers — ATS often skips content inside these.
  • Skip graphics, charts, and icons — they are invisible to ATS.
  • Use standard section headings like "Work Experience," "Education," "Skills."
  • Submit as .docx or .pdf — most modern ATS accept both, but .docx has broader compatibility.
  • Use bullet points sparingly — standard round bullets (•) are safer than symbols or arrows.

Resume Keywords Explained

ATS systems are keyword-matching engines at their core. Including the right keywords dramatically increases your ATS score and callback rate.

There are three types of resume keywords:

  • Hard Skills: Technical abilities like Python, SQL, Adobe Photoshop, financial modeling, data analysis.
  • Soft Skills: Leadership, communication, project management, collaboration, problem-solving.
  • Industry Keywords: Role-specific terms like "agile methodology," "P&L management," "SEO optimization," "full-stack development."

Always mirror the exact language from the job description. If the listing says "React.js," use "React.js" — not just "React" or "ReactJS."

Common ATS Resume Mistakes

  • Using a two-column layout that confuses parsers
  • Placing contact info in the header or footer
  • Using creative section titles like "My Journey" instead of "Work Experience"
  • Submitting as a .pages or image file
  • Not including a skills section
  • Spelling out acronyms only (ATS wants both "Search Engine Optimization" and "SEO")
  • Over-stuffing keywords unnaturally
  • Using white text to hide keywords (ATS detects this)
  • Leaving out dates for work experience
  • Not tailoring the resume per job application

Resume Optimization Tips

  • Tailor every resume to the specific job description
  • Use the exact job title somewhere in your summary or header
  • Quantify achievements: "increased sales by 34%" beats "improved sales"
  • Start bullets with strong action verbs: Led, Built, Drove, Analyzed, Delivered
  • Include both acronyms and full forms: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)"
  • Keep it to 1-2 pages — length affects ATS scoring in some systems
  • Use a professional summary that mirrors the job posting language
  • Add a dedicated Skills section with comma-separated keywords

ATS Score Improvement Strategies

If your ATS score is below 75, here are the highest-impact actions to take immediately:

  • Add a Skills section (if missing — this is the single highest-impact fix)
  • Extract keywords from the job description and weave them naturally throughout your resume
  • Rewrite your professional summary to include 5-7 target keywords
  • Ensure complete contact information is present at the top: full name, phone, email, location (city/state), LinkedIn URL
  • Add quantified achievements to each role — numbers signal competence to both ATS and recruiters
  • Remove all tables, text boxes, and graphics — reformat into clean linear text
  • Check section heading names — use conventional headings that ATS systems recognize
  • Increase keyword density — if a key skill appears once, try to include it 2-3 times naturally
  • Add certifications and tools relevant to the role, even if briefly mentioned
  • Run a custom job match check using our "Job Match" tab above to get role-specific suggestions

Research shows that resumes scoring above 80 on ATS receive a callback rate 3x higher than those below 60. Our ATS checker above will give you precise, actionable feedback to hit that threshold quickly.

The Recruiter Screening Process

Understanding how recruiters work alongside ATS helps you write a better resume. After passing ATS filtering, a real recruiter spends an average of just 7.4 seconds on an initial resume scan. They look for:

  • Job title match
  • Company brand names (previous employers)
  • Education credentials
  • Career progression clarity
  • Skills alignment

This means your resume needs to pass two tests: the ATS algorithm first, then the human eye-test. Our tool optimizes for both.

Job Search Best Practices

  • Apply within 48 hours of a job posting — first applicants get more attention
  • Use LinkedIn Easy Apply only when the application allows a resume upload
  • Customize your resume for each role — a generic resume rarely passes ATS
  • Track applications in a spreadsheet to follow up efficiently
  • Follow up with a brief email 5-7 days after applying
  • Connect with the hiring manager on LinkedIn before and after applying
  • Use niche job boards in your industry alongside Indeed and LinkedIn
  • Referrals bypass ATS entirely — always leverage your network first

ATS Resume FAQ

What is an ATS resume checker? ▾
An ATS resume checker analyzes your resume against the same criteria used by Applicant Tracking Systems that employers use to screen candidates. It checks keyword density, section completeness, formatting compliance, contact information, and overall ATS-friendliness — then gives you a score and specific recommendations to improve your chances of passing automated screening.
What is a good ATS score? ▾
Scores of 80 or above are considered ATS-friendly and have the best chance of reaching a human recruiter. Scores between 60-79 are average — you'll pass some ATS but miss others. Below 60 means significant optimization is needed. Scores of 90+ represent elite-level ATS optimization and are typical of top candidates who receive consistent callbacks.
Is this ATS checker really free? ▾
Yes — completely free, no sign-up, no email required. It runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript, meaning your resume text never leaves your device. We believe career tools should be accessible to everyone, which is why QuickTools offers all its tools at zero cost.
Does my resume data get stored or shared? ▾
No. All analysis happens entirely in your browser using client-side JavaScript. Your resume text is processed locally and is never sent to any server. When you close the browser tab, your data is gone. Privacy is not a feature here — it's just how the tool works by design.
What ATS systems does this checker simulate? ▾
Our checker is designed to simulate the parsing and scoring logic of common enterprise ATS platforms including Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, Taleo, and BambooHR. While exact scoring varies by platform, the fundamental criteria — keyword matching, section detection, formatting compliance — are consistent across all major systems.
Should I use a PDF or Word document for my resume? ▾
Both work with modern ATS systems. However, .docx files have slightly broader compatibility across older ATS platforms. If a job posting says "only PDF," use PDF. If there's no instruction, .docx is the safer choice for maximum ATS compatibility. Never submit your resume as a .pages, .jpg, or .png file — these are invisible to ATS parsers.
How often should I update my resume? ▾
Update your resume every 3-6 months, or immediately after completing a significant project, earning a certification, or gaining new skills. More importantly, customize your resume for every individual job application — a generic resume tailored to nothing rarely scores above 65 on ATS systems that compare against the job description.
