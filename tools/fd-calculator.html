What is a Fixed Deposit?

A Fixed Deposit (FD) is a financial instrument offered by banks and NBFCs that provides higher interest rates than regular savings accounts, in exchange for locking your money for a fixed period (tenure). FDs are one of the safest and most popular investment options in India.

🔒 Capital Safety

Fixed deposits are one of the safest investment options, insured up to ₹5 lakh by DICGC. Your principal is fully protected.

📈 Guaranteed Returns

Unlike market-linked instruments, FD interest rates are fixed at the time of deposit. Your returns are fully predictable.

⚡ Compound Growth

Most bank FDs compound quarterly, meaning your interest earns interest too — significantly boosting your maturity amount.

🏦 Flexible Tenures

FD tenures range from 7 days to 10 years. Senior citizens typically get an extra 0.25–0.50% interest rate on FDs.

FD Interest Calculation Formula

Most banks in India use the compound interest formula for FD maturity calculation:

A = P × ( 1 + r/n ) n×t

Frequently Asked Questions
An FD Calculator is a free online tool that computes your fixed deposit maturity amount and total interest earned based on the deposit amount, interest rate, tenure, and compounding frequency. It uses the compound interest formula A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) to instantly calculate results without any manual computation.
FD interest rates in India vary by bank and tenure. Major banks like SBI and HDFC offer 6.5–7.5% for regular customers, while small finance banks like AU Small Finance Bank offer up to 8.5–9% per annum. Senior citizens typically receive 0.25–0.50% extra. Always compare current rates on the official bank website before investing.
Yes. FD interest is taxable as "Income from Other Sources" per your applicable income tax slab. Banks deduct TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) at 10% if annual FD interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens). If your total income is below the taxable limit, you can submit Form 15G/15H to avoid TDS deduction.
Quarterly compounding compounds your interest 4 times per year, meaning each quarter's interest is added to the principal before the next quarter's interest is calculated. This results in a higher effective yield compared to annual compounding. For a 7% FD compounded quarterly, the effective annual rate (EAR) is approximately 7.19%.
Yes, most banks allow premature FD withdrawal, but a penalty of 0.5–1% is typically deducted from the applicable interest rate. For example, if your FD rate is 7% and you break it early, you may receive only 6–6.5%. Some banks offer tax-saver FDs with a 5-year lock-in period that cannot be broken prematurely.
A Tax-Saving FD is a fixed deposit with a 5-year lock-in period that qualifies for tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, up to ₹1.5 lakh per year. The interest earned is still taxable. These FDs cannot be pledged as collateral or broken prematurely.
