How It Works

Understanding Calories,
BMR & TDEE

Before you can hit your nutrition goals, you need to understand the three key numbers that drive your metabolism.

🔥

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)

BMR is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest — just to keep your heart beating, lungs breathing, and cells functioning. It's your baseline energy floor.

⚡

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

TDEE is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. It represents the total calories you actually burn in a day, including exercise, work, and daily movement.

🎯

Calorie Deficit & Surplus

Eat below TDEE to lose weight. Eat above TDEE to gain muscle. A deficit of 500 cal/day loses ~0.5 kg/week. A surplus of 250–500 cal/day builds muscle efficiently.

🧬

Macronutrients Matter

Protein builds and preserves muscle. Carbs fuel activity. Fat supports hormones and cell health. Hitting the right macro split supercharges your results beyond just calories.

The Science

Calorie Calculation Formula

We use the Mifflin-St Jeor Equation, considered the most accurate BMR formula for most adults, validated extensively in clinical research.

BMR — Male:
BMR = (10 × weight_kg) + (6.25 × height_cm) − (5 × age) + 5
BMR — Female:
BMR = (10 × weight_kg) + (6.25 × height_cm) − (5 × age) − 161
TDEE:
TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier

Sedentary: ×1.2  |  Light: ×1.375  |  Moderate: ×1.55  |  Very Active: ×1.725  |  Extreme: ×1.9

BMI Formula

BMI = weight_kg ÷ (height_m)²

Underweight: <18.5  |  Normal: 18.5–24.9  |  Overweight: 25–29.9  |  Obese: ≥30

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about calculating and using your daily calorie needs.

BMR is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest to maintain vital functions like breathing, circulation, and cell production. It represents your minimum calorie needs just to stay alive. Think of it as your engine idling.
TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. While BMR represents calories at rest, TDEE is your actual total daily burn — including exercise, walking, working, and all movement. Most people should base their calorie goals on TDEE, not BMR.
To lose weight safely, eat 500–750 calories below your TDEE per day. This creates a deficit leading to ~0.5 kg (1 lb) of fat loss per week. Never go below 1,200 calories/day for women or 1,500 for men without medical supervision, as extreme restriction can harm metabolism and muscle mass.
To build muscle, eat 250–500 calories above your TDEE daily. This calorie surplus, combined with progressive strength training and adequate protein (1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight), supports lean muscle growth while minimizing fat gain. This is called a "lean bulk."
Our calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, validated by multiple studies as the most accurate BMR formula for the general population. However, individual metabolism can vary by 10–15% based on genetics, hormones, and muscle mass. Treat results as a starting point and adjust based on 2–4 weeks of real-world data.
Macronutrients are protein, carbohydrates, and fat. Protein (4 cal/g) builds and repairs muscle. Carbs (4 cal/g) are the body's primary fuel source. Fat (9 cal/g) supports hormone production and nutrient absorption. Hitting the right macro split helps optimize body composition, energy levels, and recovery beyond just tracking total calories.
The standard BMI categories are: Underweight (<18.5), Normal weight (18.5–24.9), Overweight (25–29.9), and Obese (≥30). Note that BMI does not account for muscle mass — athletes may have a high BMI while being very lean. Use BMI alongside body fat percentage for a more complete picture.
