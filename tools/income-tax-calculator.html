How It Works
How Income Tax is Calculated in India
India uses a slab-based progressive tax system. You pay tax only on the income that falls within each slab — not your total income. Here's the step-by-step process:
STEP 01

Compute Gross Income

Add your salary/CTC, other income sources like interest, rent, capital gains, and freelance earnings.

STEP 02

Apply Deductions

Under old regime: subtract Standard Deduction (₹50,000), HRA, 80C, 80D, and other eligible deductions.

STEP 03

Arrive at Taxable Income

Gross Income – Total Deductions = Taxable Income. This is what gets taxed.

STEP 04

Apply Slab Rates

Tax is computed slab-wise at 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, or 30% on each portion of income.

STEP 05

Add Surcharge & Cess

Add 4% Health & Education Cess on the total tax. Surcharge applies for income above ₹50 lakhs.

STEP 06

Check Rebate u/s 87A

If taxable income ≤ ₹7 lakhs (new) or ₹5 lakhs (old), you get a full rebate — zero tax payable.

Tax Saving Tips
Smart Ways to Reduce Your Tax Burden
💼

Section 80C – Up to ₹1.5L

Invest in PPF, ELSS mutual funds, NSC, tax-saving FDs, LIC, or EPF to claim maximum 80C deduction.

🏥

Section 80D – Health Insurance

Pay health insurance premium for self/family (₹25,000) and parents (₹50,000 for senior citizens) for deductions.

🏠

HRA Exemption

If you live on rent, claim HRA exemption. Actual HRA, 50%/40% of basic, or actual rent minus 10% of basic — whichever is lowest.

🎓

Section 80E – Education Loan

Interest paid on education loan is fully deductible under 80E for 8 consecutive years. No upper limit.

🏦

NPS Contribution (80CCD)

Additional ₹50,000 deduction under 80CCD(1B) over and above 80C limit by investing in National Pension System.

📋

Standard Deduction

₹50,000 (old) / ₹75,000 (new) standard deduction is automatically available to all salaried employees. No proof needed.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
Which tax regime is better – old or new? ▼
It depends on your deductions. If your total deductions (80C + HRA + 80D + others) exceed roughly ₹3.75 lakhs, the old regime is usually better. Otherwise, the new regime offers lower rates without the paperwork. Use this calculator above to compare both for your specific income.
What is the basic exemption limit for FY 2026-27? ▼
Under the new tax regime, income up to ₹3,00,000 is fully exempt. Under the old tax regime, the basic exemption limit is ₹2,50,000 for individuals below 60 years, ₹3,00,000 for senior citizens (60–80 years), and ₹5,00,000 for super senior citizens (above 80 years).
Can I switch between old and new tax regime? ▼
Yes! Salaried individuals can switch between regimes every year at the time of filing ITR. However, if you have business income, you can switch from new to old only once, and returning to new regime is available once you switch back.
Is Section 80C available in the new tax regime? ▼
No. Section 80C deductions (PPF, ELSS, LIC, EPF, etc.) are not available under the new tax regime. The new regime offers lower tax rates in exchange for most deductions being disallowed. Only standard deduction of ₹75,000 is available.
What is Section 87A rebate? ▼
Section 87A provides a full tax rebate to individuals with low taxable income. Under the old regime, if taxable income ≤ ₹5 lakhs, rebate up to ₹12,500 is available. Under the new regime, if taxable income ≤ ₹7 lakhs, rebate up to ₹25,000 is available — effectively making the tax zero.
What is Health & Education Cess? ▼
A 4% Health and Education Cess is levied on the total income tax amount (after surcharge if applicable). This goes toward funding health and education initiatives. It is mandatory for all taxpayers regardless of the regime chosen.
